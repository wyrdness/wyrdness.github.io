Full Account

Beelzebub is a major demonic figure in Jewish, Christian and Islamic tradition. The name originates as Baʿal Zəvuv (בַּעַל זְבוּב, 'lord of the flies'), a deity worshipped by the Philistines at Ekron, mentioned in the Hebrew Bible in 2 Kings 1:2–3, 6, 16, where King Ahaziah of Israel sends to consult him about an injury and is rebuked by the prophet Elijah. Most scholars regard the form 'lord of the flies' as a deliberate Israelite distortion of an original Baʿal Zəvul ('exalted lord' / 'lord of the lofty house') used as a polemical pun. In the Synoptic Gospels (Mark 3:22; Matthew 10:25, 12:24–27; Luke 11:15–19), Jewish opponents accuse Jesus of casting out demons by Beelzeboul, prince of demons, and Jesus replies with the saying that 'a kingdom divided against itself cannot stand'. By the late medieval and early modern period, Beelzebub had become one of the most prominent named demons in Christian demonology, associated with gluttony in Peter Binsfeld's classification (1589) and named one of the seven princes of Hell. The Testament of Solomon (1st–3rd c. CE) names Beelzeboul as a former prince of the highest heaven now ruling the demons. Milton's Paradise Lost (1667) places him as the second-highest of the fallen angels.

Etymology

Origin:
From Biblical Hebrew בַּעַל זְבוּב (Baʿal Zəvuv), the name of a Philistine god of Ekron, transmitted through Greek βεελζεβούβ / βεελζεβούλ into Latin Beelzebub.
Meaning:
Literal Hebrew: 'lord of the flies' (zəvuv = 'fly'). Most modern scholars treat this as a polemical Hebrew distortion of an original Baʿal Zəvul, 'exalted lord' or 'prince Baal' (cf. Ugaritic zbl, 'prince', and 1 Kings 8:13 bêt-zebûl, 'lofty house'). The Greek New Testament form Beelzeboul preserves the second element.
First Use:
9th–8th century BCE: Hebrew Bible, 2 Kings 1:2–3 (referencing the cult at Ekron under King Ahaziah, c. 850 BCE)

Timeline

  • c. 850 BCE — King Ahaziah of Israel sends to consult Baʿal Zəvuv of Ekron and is rebuked by Elijah (2 Kings 1) (Earliest biblical reference)
  • c. 65 CE — Mark 3:22: Jewish scribes accuse Jesus of casting out demons by Beelzeboul, prince of demons (Establishes Beelzebub as prince of demons in Christian tradition)
  • 1st–3rd c. CE — Testament of Solomon depicts Beelzeboul as a former heavenly prince now ruling demons (Foundational pseudepigraphical demonology)
  • 1589 — Peter Binsfeld's Tractatus de confessionibus maleficorum et sagarum classifies Beelzebub as the demon of gluttony in the seven-princes-of-Hell schema (Crystallizes the deadly-sin/demon mapping)
  • 1667 — John Milton's Paradise Lost places Beelzebub as the second of the fallen angels, after Satan (Defines Beelzebub for English literary tradition)
  • 1818 — Jacques Collin de Plancy's Dictionnaire infernal includes Beelzebub among the high princes of Hell (Standardizes 19th-century iconography)
