La Diablesse

Overview

La Diablesse ('the She-Devil') is a Caribbean folkloric demoness, especially associated with Trinidad and Tobago, who appears as a beautiful woman in old-fashioned dress with one human leg and one cloven cow-hoof, luring men into the forest where they become lost and die.

Full Account

La Diablesse — Trinidadian Creole 'Lajables' or 'La Jablesse' — is a demoness of Caribbean folklore whose home tradition is in Trinidad and Tobago, with cognate figures in Grenada and the wider French-Creole Caribbean. The legend blends French-Creole, West African and Indigenous Amerindian folkloric strands. According to oral tradition, she was originally an enslaved African woman who made a pact with the Devil for eternal beauty in exchange for her soul, becoming a demi-human creature. She walks the roads at night dressed in elegant old-fashioned clothes and a wide-brimmed hat that hides her hideous face; under her long skirt one leg ends in a cloven cow-hoof. She lures unwary men — typically returning from rumshops or village dances — into the forest, where she vanishes and the victim is left to die in a ravine, river or on a precipice. She is invoked in Trinidad and Tobago as a moral cautionary figure warning against drunkenness, infidelity and night-time wandering.

Etymology

Origin:
From French la diablesse, the feminine form of le diable ('the devil'). Her Creole names — Lajables, La Jablesse — reflect Trinidadian French-Creole pronunciation.
Meaning:
'The She-Devil' / 'the female devil'.
First Use:
Embedded in 19th- and 20th-century French-Creole Caribbean oral tradition; first attested in print in 20th-century Trinidad and Tobago folklore collections.

Cultural Significance

Treated as a cautionary moral figure rather than as the object of formal worship; in rural Trinidad and Tobago belief in her remains real for many older speakers.
