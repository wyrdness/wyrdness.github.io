Al-Burāq

Overview

Al-Burāq is the celestial steed of Islamic tradition that, according to Hadith narrations, carried the Prophet Muhammad on the Isra and Mi'raj — the night journey from the Sacred Mosque in Mecca to the Al-Aqsa Mosque in Jerusalem and the subsequent ascent through the seven heavens.

Details

Origin:
A single mythological figure of universal scope within Islamic tradition. The narrative geography of the night journey runs from Mecca (al-Masjid al-Haram) to Jerusalem (al-Masjid al-Aqsa) and through the seven heavens.
Regions:
Hejaz, Levant, Jerusalem (Haram al-Sharif)
First Reported:
c. 621 CE

Full Account

Al-Burāq (Arabic: البُراق, 'the lightning' or 'the bright one') is a sacred celestial steed in Islamic tradition. According to Hadith narrations transmitted in Sahih al-Bukhari, Sahih Muslim, and other canonical collections, Al-Burāq was sent by the angel Jibrīl (Gabriel) to carry the Prophet Muhammad on the Isra and Mi'raj — the miraculous night journey from al-Masjid al-Haram (the Sacred Mosque in Mecca) to al-Masjid al-Aqsa (the Furthest Mosque, identified with the site in Jerusalem), and then the celestial ascent (mi'raj) through the seven heavens to the divine presence. Hadith descriptions of Al-Burāq are concise: 'a white animal, smaller than a mule and bigger than a donkey,' whose stride covers as far as the eye can see. Some traditions also relate that Al-Burāq carried the Prophet Ibrahim (Abraham) between Mecca and Canaan. The Quran refers to the night journey in Surah al-Isra (17:1) without naming the steed; the name Al-Burāq comes from the Hadith literature. Visual depictions, especially in Persian, Mughal, and Ottoman manuscript painting from roughly the 14th century onward, often portray Al-Burāq as a winged equine with a human (frequently feminine) face — a feature not present in early textual descriptions but widely diffused through Islamic devotional art. Sites in Jerusalem associated with the journey — the Buraq Wall (the western wall of the Haram al-Sharif/Temple Mount, where tradition holds Al-Burāq was tethered) and the Buraq Mosque — remain places of religious significance and political contestation.

Etymology

Origin:
From the Arabic root b-r-q (برق) meaning 'lightning' or 'to flash'. Some scholars (e.g., the Encyclopædia Iranica) note possible cross-influence with Middle Persian terms for a riding beast.
Meaning:
'The lightning one' — a reference to extraordinary speed.
First Use:
Attested in Hadith collections of the 9th century CE; the night journey itself is traditionally dated to c. 621 CE.

Timeline

  • c. 621 — Traditional date of the Isra and Mi'raj, in which Al-Burāq carries the Prophet Muhammad from Mecca to Jerusalem and through the seven heavens (Defining narrative event of the tradition)
  • 9th century — Hadith collections of al-Bukhari (d. 870) and Muslim ibn al-Hajjaj (d. 875) record narrations describing Al-Burāq (Canonical written documentation of the steed)
  • 1436 — Mi'rajnama (Persian, Timurid period) produced in Herat, with elaborate paintings of Al-Burāq (Major milestone in the visual tradition)
  • 1929 — Buraq Wall riots in Jerusalem (Modern political crystallisation of the religious significance of the Buraq Wall)

Cultural Significance

Lailat al-Mi'raj is observed annually on 27 Rajab in many Muslim communities, commemorating the night journey on which Al-Burāq carried the Prophet.
