Overview

The Roc (Arabic: الرُخّ, al-Rukh) is a legendary giant bird of prey from Arabian and Persian mythology, most famously described in the tales of One Thousand and One Nights, capable of carrying elephants in its talons. Its egg alone was said to measure 50 paces in circumference.

Full Account

The Roc (Arabic: الرُخّ, romanized al-Rukh; Persian rukh) is an enormous legendary bird of prey from Arabian and Persian mythology, most celebrated through its appearances in the Sinbad voyages of One Thousand and One Nights (Arabian Nights). The English term 'roc' derives from Antoine Galland's French translation of the Arabic ruḵḵ. Described as a bird of such colossal size it could carry elephants in its talons, the roc embodies the wonders of the unknown world beyond the horizon. In the Sinbad narratives, the hero encounters an enormous dome — actually a roc's egg measuring 50 paces in circumference — and escapes his predicament by securing himself to the bird's feet. Marco Polo described the roc in his accounts: 'It was for all the world like an eagle, but one indeed of enormous size; so big in fact that its quills were twelve paces long and thick in proportion.' Polo claimed the bird could carry an elephant, drop it, and consume the carcass. Chou Ch'ű-fei's 1178 work Lingwai Daida mentions 'birds large enough to use their quills as water reservoirs.' Raffia palm fronds were presented to Kublai Khan as alleged roc feathers. Scholars propose several sources for the roc legend: art historian Rudolf Wittkower traced connections to Garuda, the solar bird from Sanskrit epics (the Mahabharata and Ramayana); the pre-Islamic Arabic ˁAnqāˀ ('a giant bird with a human face and four wings') may have influenced the myth; and the extinct Aepyornis (elephant bird) of Madagascar — standing three meters tall with enormous eggs — is widely considered the most likely real-world basis for Indian Ocean roc reports. The Malagasy crowned eagle (Stephanoaetus mahery) may have been the apex avian predator of prehistoric Madagascar.

Etymology

Origin:
Arabic and Persian; the English term 'roc' derives from Antoine Galland's 18th-century French translation of the Arabic al-ruḵḵ (الرُخّ). The word entered European languages through medieval travel literature.
Meaning:
The Arabic and Persian term for the legendary giant bird; no definitive etymology of the root word is established in available sources.
First Use:
Arabic: medieval period (One Thousand and One Nights texts compiled from 9th century onward); European: Antoine Galland's translation (published 1704–1717)

Cultural Significance

In medieval Islamic cosmology and geography, the roc was treated as a real creature inhabiting the most remote parts of the world — consistent with the tradition that wonders and extraordinary beings exist at the edges of the known world.

Related Phenomena

  • Garuda — Solar bird from Sanskrit mythology (Mahabharata, Ramayana) that carries elephants; traced by art historian Rudolf Wittkower as a possible source for the roc legend
