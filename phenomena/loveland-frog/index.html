Loveland Frog

cryptid Danger: unknown Evidence: unknown

Overview

The Loveland Frog (or Frogman) is a roughly four-foot-tall bipedal humanoid with leathery skin and a frog- or lizard-like face, reported around Loveland, Ohio along the Little Miami River. It is most famous for two 1972 sightings by Loveland police officers — one of whom later identified the creature as a tailless escaped iguana.

Full Account

The Loveland Frog is a humanoid cryptid reported in and around Loveland, Ohio, along the Little Miami River. The earliest version of the legend dates to 1955, when a businessman driving on an unnamed road late at night reported seeing three roughly 3- to 4-foot-tall bipedal entities with leathery skin and frog-like faces. The creature returned to public attention in March 1972 when Loveland police officer Ray Shockey, driving on Riverside Drive near the Totes boot factory at about 1 a.m. on 3 March 1972, said he saw a 3- to 4-foot, 50- to 75-pound leathery-skinned animal cross the road in his headlights, momentarily standing upright before climbing a guardrail and descending toward the river. Roughly two weeks later a second Loveland officer, Mark Matthews, reported seeing a similar animal in the same area, shot it, recovered the body, and put it in his patrol-car trunk to show Shockey. According to Matthews, it was a large iguana approximately 3 to 3.5 feet long that had lost its tail, which is why he had not immediately recognised it. Matthews has stated that subsequent retellings of the case omitted his identification, allowing the 'Frogman' narrative to persist. The Loveland Frog is now treated by mainstream sources as an iguana misidentification flap, but it remains a regional Ohio legend and tourist motif.
