Tote-Road Shagamaw

cryptid Danger: unknown Evidence: unknown

Overview

A 'fearsome critter' of North American lumberjack folklore, first described in William T. Cox's 1910 Fearsome Creatures of the Lumberwoods. The Shagamaw has the front quarters of a bear and the hindquarters of a moose, and supposedly walks on its forelegs for exactly a quarter of a mile before switching to its hind legs.

Details

Origin:
Cox locates the Shagamaw in the Maine lumberwoods, with Aroostook County mentioned in his account.
Regions:
Maine north woods, Aroostook County, northern New England lumber camps
First Reported:
1910

Full Account

The Tote-Road Shagamaw is one of the most absurd creatures in the canon of North American 'fearsome critters' - the tall tales told in lumber camps from the late 19th century through the early 20th. It first appears in print in William T. Cox's Fearsome Creatures of the Lumberwoods, With a Few Desert and Mountain Beasts (Judd & Detweiler, Washington, D.C., 1910), illustrated by Coert Du Bois, with Latin classifications by George B. Sudworth. According to Cox, the Shagamaw was first identified by Gus Demo of Old Town, Maine, an experienced logger and trapper. The creature has the front quarters and forelegs of a bear (with paws) and the hindquarters and hind legs of a moose (with cloven hooves). After observing surveyors tracing perfectly straight lines through the forest, the Shagamaw is said to have learned to imitate them; because it could only count to 440 - the number of paces in a quarter-mile - it switches between walking on its bear forepaws and its moose hooves at exactly that interval. The result is a track that confuses woodsmen, who supposedly fall to fisticuffs accusing each other of being unable to tell a moose track from a bear track. The Shagamaw is a humorous tall tale, never represented as a real animal.

Also Known As

Etymology

Origin:
Coined or recorded by William T. Cox (1910). 'Tote road' is the woodsman's term for a logging-supply road; 'shagamaw' is of unclear derivation, possibly a fanciful word.
Meaning:
'Creature of the tote-road' - a creature seen along logging-supply trails
First Use:
1910, in Cox's Fearsome Creatures of the Lumberwoods
