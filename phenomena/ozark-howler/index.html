Full Account

The Ozark Howler is a cryptid in the folklore of the Ozark Mountains, principally reported in northern Arkansas and southern Missouri, with occasional reports from Oklahoma and eastern Texas. Tradition describes the creature as bear-sized and stocky, with a shaggy dark coat, prominent horns and glowing eyes, and a long mournful howl that gives it its name. Most documented reports date from the 20th and early 21st centuries, though enthusiasts trace the lineage of the legend to a tale that the frontiersman Daniel Boone killed an unfamiliar large hairy animal in the region in the late 18th century. The Howler has remained a regional rather than national cryptid; mainstream wildlife authorities have not endorsed any of the claimed evidence. A widely circulated set of 2015 photographs submitted to an Arkansas television station was identified by the Arkansas Game and Fish Commission as a hoax. The phenomenon is treated by folklorists as a piece of regional Ozark legend and tall-tale tradition rather than as evidence of an undescribed mammal.

Etymology

Origin:
The English name refers to the creature's signature long, mournful howl.
Meaning:
'Howler' from the reported nocturnal cry; 'Ozark' from the mountain region in which it is reported.
First Use:
Mid-to-late 20th century, in regional folklore and cryptozoology literature.

Timeline

Cultural Significance

In regional folklore the Howler is treated as an omen creature; its cry is considered an ill portent in some Ozark traditions.
