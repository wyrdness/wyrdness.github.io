Jeff the Killer

urban_legend Danger: unknown Evidence: unknown

Overview

Jeff the Killer is a creepypasta character who became one of the most widely spread internet horror figures, depicted as a disfigured pale-skinned young man with a permanent grin who sneaks into homes at night to kill, whispering 'Go to sleep.'

Full Account

Jeff the Killer is one of the most prominent characters in internet creepypasta (online horror story) tradition. The character's widely-recognized form was established in August 2011 when a story was published on the Creepypasta Wiki by user 'GamefuelTV' (Josh Jordan), written by his brother Travis Jordan. The story describes Jeffrey Woods, a teenager who moves to a new neighborhood, is attacked by bullies at a birthday party, is doused with alcohol and bleach and set on fire, and becomes obsessed with his scarred disfigurement. He carves a permanent Glasgow-smile grin into his face, removes his eyelids, and becomes a nocturnal serial killer who breaks into homes at night to murder sleeping victims while whispering his catchphrase 'Go to sleep.' Visually, the character is depicted with bone-white skin, an unnaturally wide smile scar, hollow black eye sockets (from the removed eyelids), and long black hair. An earlier version of the character was posted by 'killerjeff' (Jeff Case from Auburndale, Florida) on Newgrounds in 2006 and on DeviantArt in 2011, though the widely-spread story is the 2011 Creepypasta Wiki version. Jeff the Killer became one of the 'big three' creepypasta characters alongside Slender Man and the Rake, generating numerous derivative characters including Jane the Killer, Homicidal Liu, and Nina the Killer. In April 2017, a real-world incident in Urbana, Ohio involved a 14-year-old who claimed a 'Jeff' alternate personality during a fatal stabbing; he was convicted of aggravated murder in 2019.

Cultural Significance

Within creepypasta fan communities, Jeff the Killer is treated as a fictional character, though some young or vulnerable readers have reported genuinely fearing the character. The 2017 Ohio murder case demonstrates the rarest and most serious end of the spectrum of real-world impact.
