Van Meter Visitor

cryptid Danger: unknown Evidence: unknown

Overview

The Van Meter Visitor is a winged creature reported by multiple witnesses in Van Meter, Iowa over five consecutive nights in late September to early October 1903; witnesses described a large, dark, winged humanoid with a bioluminescent horn projecting from its forehead and a powerful offensive odor.

Details

Origin:
Single location: Van Meter, Iowa, USA. No other sightings documented in the area before or after the 1903 incidents.
Regions:
Van Meter, Dallas County, Iowa
First Reported:
1903-09-29

Full Account

The Van Meter Visitor refers to a series of creature sightings in the small town of Van Meter, Iowa (population approximately 800 in 1903) over five consecutive nights beginning September 29, 1903. The accounts were reported by multiple respected community members — including a local businessman (Ulysses G. Griffith), a bank cashier (Clarence Dunn), and other identified townspeople — who independently described an identical creature: a large, winged, dark-colored being with a distinctive horn or projection on its forehead that emitted a bright, powerful beam of light described as 'as bright as an electric headlight.' The creature also reportedly emitted a powerful offensive odor. Witnesses who fired at the creature reported that their shots had no apparent effect. The encounters escalated over five nights, with multiple groups of townspeople confronting the creature. The climactic encounter occurred at an abandoned coal mine shaft on the outskirts of town, where the creature (and reportedly a second specimen) disappeared into the mine and was not seen again. Three-toed footprints found near the mine were documented and apparently cast in plaster. The 1903 sightings were covered by contemporary Iowa newspapers including the Des Moines Daily News, providing documentary evidence of the immediate reporting. The case was largely forgotten until the early 21st century when researchers — notably Chad Lewis, Noah Voss, and Kevin Lee Nelson — rediscovered the original newspaper accounts and published them (The Van Meter Visitor, 2013). Van Meter now celebrates its cryptid heritage with an annual festival.

Etymology

Origin:
Named after Van Meter, Iowa, the small town where the sightings occurred in 1903.
Meaning:
'Van Meter Visitor' was not a contemporary 1903 term; it was coined by later researchers. Contemporary newspaper reports described 'the monster' or 'the creature.'
First Use:
1903 newspaper reports (contemporary); 'Van Meter Visitor' as a named phenomenon: 21st century, popularized by Lewis, Voss, and Nelson (2013)

Timeline

  • 1903-09-29 — First sighting: local businessman Ulysses G. Griffith notices a powerful light coming from atop a building; encounters a large winged creature with a beam of light projecting from its forehead (First documented encounter with the Van Meter Visitor)
  • 1903-09-30 — Bank cashier Clarence Dunn awoken by a bright light entering his home; goes outside armed with a gun, fires at the creature; bullets have no effect (Second documented encounter; first documented gunfire at the creature)
  • 1903-10-01 — Three-toed, unusually large footprints found near an abandoned mine shaft outside of town; reportedly cast in plaster by witnesses (Only physical trace evidence from the events)
  • 1903-10-03 — Multiple armed townspeople confront the creature at the abandoned mine shaft; two specimens observed; all gunfire ineffective; both creatures retreat into the mine and are never seen again (Final and most dramatic encounter; end of the Van Meter Visitor events)
  • 2013-01-01 — Chad Lewis, Noah Voss, and Kevin Lee Nelson publish The Van Meter Visitor: A True and Mysterious Encounter with the Unknown; bring the 1903 events to wide attention by presenting the original newspaper accounts (Revival of the case for modern audiences; primary research source presenting original documentation)

Cultural Significance

Within Van Meter, the 1903 events are now embraced as authentic local history and a source of civic pride. The annual festival celebrates the creature as a local icon.
