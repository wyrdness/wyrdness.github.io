Hantu Jerangan

entity_spirit Danger: unknown Evidence: unknown

Overview

Hantu Jerangan is a regional Malay fishing-community spirit said to be associated with fishing nets and unexplained drownings at sea. It is a relatively obscure figure within the broader hantu taxonomy and is documented mainly in coastal Malaysian oral tradition rather than in major published ethnographies.

Details

Origin:
Coastal Malaysian fishing communities; not well documented in academic sources outside this region.
Regions:
Coastal Peninsular Malaysia

Modern oral tradition

Full Account

Hantu Jerangan is a Malay folkloric spirit reported in fishing communities along Malaysia's coasts. As with other hantu laut (sea spirits) and hantu-air-related entities, it functions as a supernatural explanation for unexplained drownings, lost fishermen, and dangerous conditions at sea, and reinforces traditional taboos and protective practices around fishing. The figure is distinct from better-attested Malay maritime spirits such as Hantu Laut, Hantu Jerambang, and Hantu Balung Bidai, but is commonly grouped with them as part of the broader Malay water-spirit complex. Available documentation in mainstream English-language sources is thin; most references are confined to community-level oral tradition and Malaysian-language folklore reportage. Where it is described, the entity is associated specifically with the dangers of fishing-net work and is invoked to caution fishermen against tangling, working alone, or transgressing customary maritime taboos.

Etymology

Origin:
Malay: hantu ('ghost' or 'spirit'); 'jerangan' is reported in coastal-community usage in connection with nets/fishing-gear contexts. Precise lexical history not well documented.
Meaning:
Reported as 'fishing-net ghost' in popular English sources; the precise Malay sense is uncertain in published references.
First Use:
Not documented in major ethnographies; recorded in modern Malaysian folkloric reportage.
