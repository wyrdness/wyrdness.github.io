The Hookman

urban_legend Danger: unknown Evidence: unknown

Overview

A classic American urban legend in which a couple parked at a lovers' lane flee from a radio-warned escaped killer with a hook for a hand and find his hook torn off and dangling from the car door handle.

Details

Origin:
American urban legend with countless local variants throughout the contiguous United States; the legend has also circulated in Canada and the United Kingdom.
Regions:
United States — broadly distributed
First Reported:
1960-11-08

Full Account

The Hookman (also called 'The Hook' or 'The Hook Man') is a classic American urban legend that began circulating in the 1950s and was widely told among teenagers by 1959. In its standard form, a young couple is parked at a remote lovers' lane when a radio bulletin announces that a dangerous prisoner — variously a serial killer or escaped patient from a mental institution — has just escaped, identifiable by a hook in place of one hand. The girl insists they leave; the boy reluctantly drives off. When they arrive home and the boy walks around to open her door, they find a bloody hook hanging from the handle. The legend was first published, so far as documented, in the 'Dear Abby' advice column on 8 November 1960, and was treated as a foundational example by folklorists including Jan Harold Brunvand, who placed it at the centre of his 1981 The Vanishing Hitchhiker. Folklorists have read the legend as a coded warning to teenagers about isolated places and adolescent sexuality (Alan Dundes), as a contest between conventional and deviant social actors (Bengt af Klintberg), and as a moralistic 'custodian' tale (Bill Ellis). The Hookman has been a recurring template in modern horror cinema and television, including a hook-wielding killer in I Know What You Did Last Summer (1997) and episodes of Supernatural and Community.

Etymology

Origin:
American English. The character is identified by his prosthetic hook in place of a hand; 'hookman' is a descriptive nickname rather than a proper name.
Meaning:
'The man with the hook'; a hook-handed killer character in an urban-legend script.
First Use:
Circulating orally in the United States from the early-to-mid 1950s; first documented in print in 1960 in the 'Dear Abby' newspaper column.
