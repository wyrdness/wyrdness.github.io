Reptilian Conspiracy Theory

conspiracy_theory Danger: unknown Evidence: unknown

Overview

A modern conspiracy theory that alleges shape-shifting reptilian humanoid beings secretly control Earth's institutions, popularised by British conspiracy author David Icke from the late 1990s onwards.

Details

Origin:
As a conspiracy belief, the theory has audiences in over 40 countries, with English-language strongholds in the UK, US, Canada, Australia, and online communities globally. Belief has been measured most directly in the United States.
Regions:
English-speaking online conspiracist communities

1929-08-01

Full Account

The reptilian conspiracy theory is a modern conspiracy belief, popularised by the British conspiracy author David Icke, that the world is secretly ruled by a hidden elite of shape-shifting reptilian humanoids — sometimes said to originate from the Alpha Draconis star system — who occupy positions of high power including monarchies, banking dynasties, and political offices. The theory in its current form coalesces in Icke's 1999 book The Biggest Secret, drawing on earlier pulp-fiction reptilian motifs (Robert E. Howard's 1929 'The Shadow Kingdom'), Theosophical 'Lemurian' speculation in Helena Blavatsky's The Secret Doctrine (1888), and the UFO-contact and Anunnaki literatures of the 1980s and 1990s. Scholars including Michael Barkun and Edward Guimont have documented its incorporation of older antisemitic conspiracy tropes, particularly motifs from the fabricated Protocols of the Elders of Zion. A 2013 Public Policy Polling survey found that approximately 4% of registered American voters expressed agreement with reptilian-rule ideas; the theory is widely considered a textbook example of modern internet-era conspiracism.

Etymology

Origin:
Modern English coinage drawing on Latin reptilis ('crawling, creeping'), via the herpetological term 'reptile' applied to alleged humanoid beings.
Meaning:
'Reptilian' = of, or resembling, a reptile; here referring to alleged humanoid lizard-like beings.
First Use:
Reptilian humanoids appear in Robert E. Howard's pulp story 'The Shadow Kingdom' (Weird Tales, August 1929). The modern conspiracy framing is associated with David Icke from the mid-1990s, particularly The Biggest Secret (1999).

Timeline

  • 1888-01-01 — Helena Blavatsky publishes The Secret Doctrine, with 'dragon-men' on Lemuria (Theosophical antecedent of reptilian motifs)
  • 1929-08-01 — Robert E. Howard's 'The Shadow Kingdom' published in Weird Tales, introducing shape-shifting serpent men (Direct pulp-fiction antecedent identified by Michael Barkun)
  • 1976-01-01 — Zecharia Sitchin publishes The 12th Planet, popularising the Anunnaki ancient-astronaut narrative (Pseudohistorical framework later co-opted by reptilian theorists)
  • 1995-01-01 — David Icke publishes ...And the Truth Shall Set You Free, introducing reptilian themes (First Icke book to substantially incorporate reptilian conspiracy)
  • 1999-01-01 — David Icke publishes The Biggest Secret, the canonical statement of his reptilian theory (Defining text of the modern reptilian conspiracy theory)
  • 2003-09-30 — Michael Barkun publishes A Culture of Conspiracy, including the foundational scholarly analysis of Icke's reptilian theory and its antisemitic affinities (First major academic treatment)
  • 2013-04-02 — Public Policy Polling survey finds approximately 4% of U.S. registered voters express agreement with the reptilian-elite idea (Headline measurement of belief prevalence)
