Nian

Overview

A beast of Chinese folklore said to emerge at the end of each lunar year to attack villages, scared off by the colour red, fire, and loud noises — the etiological story for many Chinese New Year traditions.

Details

Origin:
Greater China and the global Chinese diaspora; the Nian is a pan-Chinese folkloric figure attached to Lunar New Year customs.
Regions:
Greater China, Chinese diaspora communities worldwide
First Reported:
Early 20th century

Full Account

The Nian (年獸, 'Year Beast') is a creature of Chinese popular folklore traditionally said to emerge at the end of each lunar year to raid villages for food, devouring crops, livestock, and people. According to the legend, villagers discovered that the Nian fears three things — the colour red, loud noises (drums, firecrackers, clashing plates), and fire — and these discoveries became the basis of enduring Chinese New Year customs: red couplets and lanterns, firecrackers, and bright lights. The Chinese name for celebrating New Year, guo nian (過年), is sometimes glossed as 'pass over Nian' or 'overcome Nian'. While the Nian is widely treated as a traditional creature, written sources referring to a creature called nian appear only from the early 20th century, and Wikipedia and other reference works flag the depth of its traditional pedigree as uncertain.

Cultural Significance

Popular Chinese tradition holds that the protective customs of the Lunar New Year originated as defences against the Nian, although the historical record for the creature itself is shallow.
