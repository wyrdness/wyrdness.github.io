Hasshaku-sama

Overview

Hasshaku-sama is a Japanese internet-era urban legend originating on 2channel describing an unnaturally tall female ghost roughly 2.4 metres in height who, having become fixated on a child, pursues them with a deep masculine 'po, po, po' laugh and an ability to imitate family members' voices.

Full Account

Hasshaku-sama (八尺様, 'Lady Eight Shaku', sometimes romanized Hachishakusama) is a modern Japanese urban legend that originated as a creepypasta-style ghost story on the anonymous Japanese imageboard 2channel (2chan). The traditional account is told as the recollection of a teenage boy who, while staying at his grandparents' rural house, sees an extraordinarily tall woman in a flowing white dress and a wide-brimmed sun hat looking over the garden wall and emitting a deep, hollow 'po, po, po' laugh. His grandfather recognises the description as Hasshaku-sama and explains that once she has fixed her attention on a child she will pursue them, that she is able to mimic the voices of family members to lure the victim out, and that the family must hide the child within a salt-protected room while a Buddhist priest is summoned. Her name describes her height: hasshaku ('eight shaku') is roughly 240 cm (about 7 ft 10 in / nearly 8 ft) using the historical Japanese length measure (1 shaku ≈ 30.3 cm). Hasshaku-sama emerged in the 2000s and is one of the most influential 2channel-born ghost legends, frequently illustrated in fan art, manga, and short film, and frequently re-told and adapted in Japanese and global creepypasta communities.

Etymology

Origin:
Japanese internet folklore on 2channel; the name is built from hasshaku (eight shaku, an old Japanese length measure) + the honorific -sama.
Meaning:
'Lady Eight Shaku' — referring to the entity's signature unnatural height of approximately 2.4 metres.
First Use:
Popularised on 2channel in the mid-2000s; commonly cited as having appeared in 2008 in the form of the now-canonical greenhouse story.
