Owlman

cryptid Danger: unknown Evidence: unknown

Overview

A winged humanoid cryptid reported around the parish church of St Mawnan and St Stephen in Cornwall, England, in 1976 and a handful of subsequent years. The case is heavily entangled with the self-described wizard and showman Tony 'Doc' Shiels, and is widely considered a hoax or folkloric construction.

Origin:
Confined to the immediate vicinity of the parish church of St Mawnan and St Stephen, near Mawnan Smith on the Helford River in south Cornwall, England.

1976-04-17

Full Account

The Owlman of Mawnan is a winged humanoid cryptid associated with the wooded coastal churchyard of St Mawnan and St Stephen near the village of Mawnan Smith on the Helford River, Cornwall, England. The case was opened by sisters June (12) and Vicky (9) Melling on 17 April 1976, who reported seeing 'a large winged creature' hovering above the church tower; their father Don abruptly ended their family holiday after hearing the account, and the artist Tony 'Doc' Shiels collected a drawing from one of the girls and named the creature 'Owlman'. A second cluster of reports followed on 3 July 1976, when 14-year-olds Sally Chapman and Barbara Perry, camping in woods nearby, claimed to have seen 'a big owl with pointed ears, as big as a man', with glowing eyes and black pincer-like claws. Sporadic further reports were filed in 1978, 1979, 1989 and 1995, almost all collected by Shiels or by the cryptozoologist Jonathan Downes, who began investigating the case in 1989 and published The Owlman and Others in 1997. Witnesses describe a roughly 1.5-metre-tall figure with feathers, large wings, a flat owl-like face, pointed ears, red glowing eyes and clawed feet. The phenomenon has been compared, by Shiels and Downes among others, to the Mothman of Point Pleasant, West Virginia. Sceptics including occult historian Gareth Medway argue that the case has the hallmarks of a Shiels-orchestrated hoax: every detailed witness was either Shiels himself, a member of his circle, an anonymous letter-writer to the press, or someone who reported only to Shiels and was never independently interviewed. Mundane candidates include the Eurasian eagle-owl (Bubo bubo), occasional escapees of which have settled in the British countryside, and large barn owls or short-eared owls roosting in the church tower.

Timeline
