Douen

entity_spirit Danger: unknown Evidence: unknown

Overview

In the folklore of Trinidad and Tobago and other parts of the Eastern Caribbean, the douen is the spirit of a child who died before being baptized; depicted as a small, faceless figure with backward-facing feet and a wide straw hat, said to lure other children into the forest.

Full Account

The douen (also spelled dwen in Saint Lucia) is a folkloric spirit-child of Trinidad and Tobago. According to tradition, douens are the souls of children who died before being baptized, condemned to wander the forests as small figures with no face except for a small mouth, with feet that face backwards (so their tracks lead in the opposite direction of travel), and wearing oversized straw hats that hide their featurelessness. They are said to call to living children using a parent's voice, attempting to lure them into the bush where they become hopelessly lost. The folklore is broadly understood as primarily of African foundation, with French, Spanish, and English colonial influences, and is sometimes connected by folklorists to the Mayan Tata Duende and the Iberian/Latin American duende. Douens remain a vital part of Trinidadian cultural identity, appearing in school curricula, children's literature, and Carnival traditions, and shaping practical advice (parents traditionally caution against calling children's names aloud near forests).

Cultural Significance

Parents traditionally avoid calling children's names aloud in or near the forest; failing to baptize a child promptly is said to risk the child becoming a douen.
