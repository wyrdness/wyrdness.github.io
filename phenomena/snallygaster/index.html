Snallygaster

cryptid Danger: unknown Evidence: unknown

Overview

The Snallygaster is a dragon-like creature of central-Maryland folklore — particularly Frederick County and the South Mountain area — whose name derives from the Pennsylvania German 'schneller geist' ('quick spirit'). The creature became a newspaper sensation in 1909 when the Middletown Valley Register published a series of sensational reports.

Full Account

The Snallygaster is a regional Maryland cryptid associated with Frederick County, the Middletown Valley, South Mountain, and the wider Blue Ridge area. Its name is widely traced to the Pennsylvania German phrase 'schneller geist', meaning 'quick spirit' or 'quick ghost', a usage brought to Maryland by 18th-century German-speaking settlers. Early local tradition described a winged, beak-toothed creature that snatched livestock and frightened children; on barns in the region, seven-pointed hex stars were sometimes painted as protective signs. The creature became a national sensation in February–March 1909 when the Middletown Valley Register, edited by George C. Rhoderick with reporter Ralph S. Wolfe, ran a series of articles describing dramatic Snallygaster encounters in central Maryland. Press coverage claimed that the Smithsonian Institution had offered a reward for the hide and that President Theodore Roosevelt had considered postponing an African safari to hunt the creature. The campaign was eventually revealed as a circulation-driving hoax. Periodic Snallygaster newspaper revivals followed, including a 1932 wave reporting that the creature had drowned in a moonshine vat. In modern reception the Snallygaster is a beloved Maryland regional symbol, lending its name to the Snallygaster beer-and-music festival and appearing in J. K. Rowling's 'Fantastic Beasts and Where to Find Them' (2017) and in 'Fallout 76' (2018).
