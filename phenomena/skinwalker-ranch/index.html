Skinwalker Ranch

location Danger: unknown Evidence: unknown

Overview

Skinwalker Ranch is a 512-acre property in Uintah County, Utah, that has been the subject of claims of extensive paranormal activity including UFO sightings, cattle mutilations, poltergeist phenomena, and cryptid encounters, and which was investigated by Robert Bigelow's NIDS organization and a $22 million Pentagon study.

Full Account

Skinwalker Ranch is approximately 512 acres of private land located southeast of Ballard in Uintah County, Utah, bordering the Uintah and Ouray Indian Reservation. The property first attracted national attention during the ownership of Terry and Gwen Sherman (1994–1996), who reported close to 100 incidents involving cattle mutilations, UFO and orb sightings, large unidentified animals with red eyes, and other anomalous phenomena. The Sherman family sold the ranch in 1996 to Robert Bigelow's National Institute for Discovery Science (NIDSci) for $200,000. NIDSci conducted approximately eight years of scientific monitoring but encountered significant difficulty obtaining evidence consistent with scientific publication. The ranch subsequently connected to the Defense Intelligence Agency when DIA official James Lacatski visited; this connection, along with the advocacy of Senator Harry Reid and Senator Ted Stevens, helped secure approximately $22 million in Department of Defense funding (through the AAWSAP program) for research into unidentified aerial phenomena. In 2016, Brandon Fugal purchased the property through Adamantium Real Estate LLC for approximately $500,000. The ranch has been the subject of a History Channel reality television series (The Secret of Skinwalker Ranch, 2020–present). Skeptics, including Robert Sheaffer, argue the phenomena are illusory, noting that prior 60-year residents reported no supernatural events and that NIDSci produced no scientific publications supporting the claims. No peer-reviewed scientific evidence substantiating the paranormal claims has been published.

Etymology

Origin:
Named after the Navajo/Ute concept of the skinwalker (yee naaldlooshii) — a witch or shape-shifter in Navajo tradition — by popular writers and media, not by the Uintah-area Native nations themselves.
Meaning:
A ranch associated in popular culture with skinwalker legends; the name is an outsider imposition and does not reflect the Uintah and Ouray Ute Nation's own traditions or naming.
First Use:
The 'Skinwalker Ranch' name was popularized in the 1996 Deseret News and Las Vegas Mercury articles and the 2005 book Hunt for the Skinwalker by Colm Kelleher and George Knapp.

Cultural Significance

The ranch operates within the American UFO subculture as a symbol of government awareness of and concealment of paranormal phenomena. Proponents interpret the AAWSAP funding as implicit official acknowledgment of genuine anomalies.

Related Phenomena
