Gozu (Cow Head)

urban_legend Danger: unknown Evidence: unknown

Overview

Gozu ('Cow Head') is a Japanese urban legend about a story so terrifying that anyone who hears it is said to die or fall gravely ill. The full text of the story is itself never told.

Details

Origin:
Originated and primarily circulated in Japan; now diffused globally through internet 'creepypasta' and horror communities.
Regions:
Japan, Japanese-language internet
First Reported:
20th century

Full Account

Gozu (牛の首, Ushi no Kubi, 'Cow's Head') is a self-referential Japanese urban legend: a story whose alleged content is so horrifying that hearing or reading it causes the listener to tremble uncontrollably for days and ultimately to die. By the legend's own logic, the actual narrative cannot be transmitted; only the framing legend about it survives. The most popular modern frame describes a 1960s/1980s schoolteacher on a bus trip who, against the protests of his students, begins to tell the 'Gozu' tale; he then enters a trance, and when he comes to, every child on the bus is unconscious and the bus has crashed. Gozu has at times been rumoured to be an unpublished work by the Japanese science-fiction author Sakyo Komatsu (1931–2011), although there is no documentary evidence linking him to the legend. A separate, unrelated Ukrainian folktale also titled 'Cow's Head' exists in which a disembodied cow's head rewards a kind woman, and Buddhist mythology has the entirely separate Gozu (牛頭) and Mezu (馬頭), ox- and horse-headed guardians of hell.

Etymology

Origin:
Japanese 牛の首 (ushi no kubi) — 'cow's head' or 'cow's neck'. Also written/read as Gozu (牛頭, lit. 'ox-head'), homophonous with the name of the Buddhist hell-guardian.
Meaning:
Cow's Head / Ox Head
First Use:
Mid- to late-20th century in Japanese popular folklore

Timeline

  • 20th century — Legend circulates in Japanese oral tradition and schoolyard folklore (Establishes the meta-narrative form of the legend)
  • Late 20th–early 21st century — Legend appears in Japanese print compendia of urban legends and on Japanese horror websites (Codifies the school-bus frame and the Komatsu rumour)
  • 2003-07-12 — Takashi Miike's surrealist film 'Gozu' (極道恐怖大劇場 牛頭) is released; though unrelated to the urban legend, it shares the name (Reinforces popular association of the name 'Gozu' with horror)
