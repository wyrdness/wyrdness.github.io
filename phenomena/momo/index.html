Full Account

Momo, an abbreviation of Missouri Monster, is a regional Bigfoot-type cryptid associated with the area around Louisiana, Missouri, in northeastern Missouri (Pike County) along the Mississippi River. Witnesses described a large, hair-covered, bipedal creature roughly seven feet tall, with a 'pumpkin-shaped' head, glowing orange eyes, and a strong putrid smell. The most widely publicised sightings occurred in July 1972, including a 11 July 1972 incident in which two young brothers playing in their backyard and their older sister, who saw the creature from the kitchen window, reported a hairy creature carrying a dead dog. Local fire chief and city council member Richard Allan Murray was among other witnesses. A roughly 20-person posse searched the area but found no specimen. The active sighting period was brief and Momo became a fixture of regional folklore and 1970s American 'monster flap' culture, including a Six Flags theme-park ride (1973-1994) and a 2019 docudrama film.

Momo is broadly treated in popular culture as a regional Bigfoot variant; locally it functions both as folk creature and as town mascot.
