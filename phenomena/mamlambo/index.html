Mamlambo

Overview

A river goddess and serpent water spirit of South African (especially Zulu and Xhosa) tradition, said to grant wealth to those who keep her — and to take a heavy toll from those who do.

Full Account

Mamlambo is a river deity in southern African (particularly Zulu and Xhosa) folklore, often described as a long, snake-like creature inhabiting rivers and pools. She is associated with wealth and prosperity — a person who 'keeps' a Mamlambo may become rich, but the spirit demands a price, often the deaths of family members or the keeper's social isolation. In 1997, South African newspapers (including the Star and Cape Argus) reported a wave of fears around the Mzintlava River near Mount Ayliff in the Eastern Cape, where bodies recovered from the river had been disfigured; the disfigurement was attributed by police to post-mortem crab damage but by local people to a creature they called the 'Brain Sucker' and identified with Mamlambo. The scholar Felicity Wood has analyzed the modern Mamlambo cult as expressing anxieties about Western prosperity, individualism, and the disruption of communal life.
