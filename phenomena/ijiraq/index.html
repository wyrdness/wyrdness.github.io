Ijiraq

entity_spirit Danger: unknown Evidence: unknown

Overview

The ijiraq is a shapeshifting being of Inuit religion and oral tradition, said to live between the worlds of the living and the dead, glimpsed only at the edge of vision and known to take and hide away children.

Details

Origin:
The ijiraq is part of Inuit religion and folklore, especially among the Inuit of the eastern Canadian Arctic, the central Arctic islands, and the high Arctic. The term is well-attested in North Baffin Inuktitut.
Regions:
Nunavut, Baffin Island, High Arctic, Inuit Nunangat, Bathurst Island
First Reported:
Pre-contact

Full Account

The ijiraq (plural ijirait) is a class of spirit being in Inuit religion, especially well-attested in North Baffin Inuktitut, where the term carries the gloss 'Shape Shifter'. Ijirait inhabit a liminal place 'between two worlds; not quite inside this one, nor quite out of it', and are said to be the souls of people who travelled too far north hunting and became trapped between the realm of the living and the dead. They can take any form — caribou, bear, fox, or human — but in any disguise their eyes remain red, their mouth and eyes are set sideways, and they cannot be seen directly: they appear at the edge of vision and are forgotten almost as soon as they are encountered. They sometimes lead travellers astray and are particularly known for stealing children and abandoning them in the tundra. The ijiraq is one of several beings in Inuit cosmology that mark the danger of the polar wilderness, alongside the tariaksuq (half-man, half-caribou), the qallupilluit (water-creatures who steal disobedient children), and the tupilaq.

Etymology

Origin:
Inuktitut, especially North Baffin dialects, where the term means 'shape shifter'.
Meaning:
Shape shifter; one who appears at the edge of vision; a being-between-worlds.
First Use:
Inuit oral tradition. The earliest sustained ethnographic English-language documentation of Inuit cosmology dates to the late 19th century (Boas, Rasmussen).

Cultural Significance

The ijiraq is a real being whose existence is taken seriously in many Inuit communities; encounters require care, not mockery, and stolen children can be recovered through committed family effort and community vigilance.
