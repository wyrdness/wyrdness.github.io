Min Min Light

atmospheric_phenomenon Danger: unknown Evidence: unknown

Overview

An anomalous light phenomenon reported across the Australian outback — most famously near Boulia in Queensland's Channel Country — and now widely attributed by physicist Jack Pettigrew (2003) to an inferior-mirage Fata Morgana effect over cool, still inland air.

Details

Origin:
Reports range across outback Australia from western New South Wales (Brewarrina) north to Boulia in northern Queensland, with additional sightings in Yunta (South Australia) and the Pilbara (Western Australia). The classic location is the Channel Country of western Queensland.
Regions:
Channel Country (western Queensland), Outback Queensland, Western New South Wales, South Australia, Pilbara, Western Australia
First Reported:
Pre-contact (Aboriginal traditions); reported in European-language sources from the late 19th c.

Full Account

The Min Min light is an unexplained light phenomenon reported across the Australian outback. The classic location is the Channel Country of western Queensland — particularly the Boulia–Winton area on the road between Boulia and Winton — but reports extend from Brewarrina in western New South Wales to as far north as Boulia, and to Yunta in South Australia and the Pilbara in Western Australia. Witnesses describe fuzzy, disc-shaped lights, usually white but sometimes coloured, that hover just above the horizon, follow vehicles or people at a constant distance, and disappear when approached or fired on. In a March 2003 paper in Clinical and Experimental Optometry, neuroscientist Jack Pettigrew of the University of Queensland argued that the Min Min light is a Fata Morgana mirage in which distant lights — homestead windows, car headlights, even campfires — are refracted by a strong temperature inversion over the cold ground of inland Australia, appearing close, strange, and persistent. Aboriginal traditions of the area incorporate the lights in their own terms; the name itself is sometimes traced to the Min Min Hotel ruins between Boulia and Winton, but neither this nor an Aboriginal-language origin has been firmly established.

Etymology

Origin:
English Australian; the name's origin is uncertain. Wikipedia notes possible derivation from the Min Min Hotel (a former staging post between Boulia and Winton, near which a 1918 sighting was reported) or from an Aboriginal-language word, but neither connection has been substantiated.

Cultural Significance

Within outback culture the Min Min light is widely treated as a real (if unexplained) experience; the Pettigrew hypothesis has now joined folklore as the most-cited explanation.
