Duende

fae_folklore Danger: unknown Evidence: unknown

Overview

Duendes are small humanoid folkloric beings of Iberian, Latin American, and Filipino tradition, ranging from helpful or mischievous house-sprites to dangerous forest dwellers said to lure children astray; in 1933 Federico García Lorca repurposed the word as an aesthetic concept for the dark vital force of art.

Details

Origin:
Iberian Peninsula, Latin America (especially Mexico, Central America, and Andean countries), the Philippines, and the Mariana Islands; with parallels and overlaps with regional Indigenous traditions.
Regions:
Cantabria and Asturias, Galicia, Andalusia, Yucatán Peninsula, Oaxaca cloud forests, Belizean Cayo and Toledo districts, Andean cloud forests, Philippine archipelago
First Reported:
Medieval
