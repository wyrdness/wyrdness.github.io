Waverly Hills Sanatorium

location Danger: unknown Evidence: unknown

Overview

A former tuberculosis sanatorium in Louisville, Kentucky (opened 1910, expanded 1926, closed 1961) widely reputed to be haunted; among the most heavily investigated 'haunted hospital' locations in the United States.

Details

Origin:
A single fixed location: the Waverly Hills Sanatorium complex on Paralee Drive, Louisville, Kentucky.
Regions:
Louisville, Kentucky, Jefferson County, Kentucky
First Reported:
1910-01-01

Full Account

Waverly Hills Sanatorium is a former tuberculosis hospital in the Waverly Hills neighborhood of Louisville, Kentucky. The original two-story wooden facility opened in 1910 and was replaced by a much larger five-story Gothic Revival brick building that opened on October 17, 1926. Following the introduction of the antibiotic streptomycin in 1943 and improvements in TB treatment, the sanatorium closed in June 1961; the building reopened the next year as the Woodhaven Geriatric Center, which the Commonwealth of Kentucky shut down in 1980/1982. Since being purchased by Tina and Charlie Mattingly in 2001, the site has been preserved as a paranormal-tourism attraction and a Halloween haunted-attraction venue, with proceeds going toward restoration. Waverly Hills was added to the U.S. National Register of Historic Places on July 12, 1983. Its reputation as a haunted location is sustained by reports of full-bodied apparitions, shadow figures, disembodied voices, and physical contact, and by appearances on television programmes including Ghost Hunters and Ghost Adventures and in the 2005 horror film Death Tunnel. The building includes a service tunnel — the so-called 'body chute' or 'death tunnel' — that connected the heating plant to the main building and is the focus of much of the site's modern legend, although mainstream historical accounts describe it as a service and supply tunnel that may also have been used to discreetly remove the bodies of deceased patients.

Etymology

Origin:
Named for the Waverly Hills neighborhood of Louisville; the area was reportedly named for Sir Walter Scott's Waverley novels by an early-19th-century schoolteacher, Major Thomas H. Hays.
Meaning:
Place-name derived from the early-19th-century literary Waverley association.
First Use:
The sanatorium opened under the name Waverly Hills in 1910.

Cultural Significance

Local and online communities widely treat Waverly Hills as one of the most active haunted locations in the United States; mainstream history and skeptical scholarship treat the haunting reputation as cultural rather than evidential.
