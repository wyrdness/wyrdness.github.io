The Russian Sleep Experiment

urban_legend Danger: unknown Evidence: unknown

Overview

A widely-shared internet horror story, posted to the Creepypasta Wiki on 10 August 2010, framed as a declassified record of a Soviet experiment in which prisoners kept awake for 15 days by a stimulant gas descend into psychotic violence. It is fiction; investigations by Snopes and others have repeatedly confirmed there is no such Soviet experiment.

Full Account

'The Russian Sleep Experiment' is one of the most-shared horror creepypastas of the early internet era. Originally posted on 10 August 2010 to the Creepypasta Wiki by a user with the handle 'OrangeSoda', the story claims to be a leaked record of a Soviet research project conducted between 1940 and 1951 in which five political prisoners are kept awake for fifteen days by a stimulant gas, sealed in a chamber and observed via microphone and one-way glass. The text describes the subjects' progressive psychosis, paranoid hallucinations, refusal to be allowed to sleep, self-mutilation, evisceration, and apparent transformation into superhumanly strong, near-immortal creatures. The story circulated on Reddit, YouTube narrations, Tumblr and link-aggregators, and is widely re-shared as if it were a genuine Soviet record. It is, however, fiction: there is no archival or scientific evidence of such an experiment, and Snopes, news.com.au, LiveAbout and the Creepypasta Wiki itself classify it as fabricated. The story has nonetheless been hugely influential, generating multiple novels and at least two feature-film adaptations and serving as a paradigm case of mistaken-for-true internet folklore in academic discussions of digital legend.
