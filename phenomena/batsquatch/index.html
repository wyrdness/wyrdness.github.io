Batsquatch

cryptid Danger: unknown Evidence: unknown

Overview

A winged, ape-like cryptid said to inhabit the area around Mount St. Helens and adjacent forests of western Washington. Best known from a 1994 sighting by Brian Canfield in Pierce County.

Details

Origin:
Reports center on the southern Cascades of Washington State, particularly around Mount St. Helens and Mount Rainier in Pierce, Skamania, and Lewis counties.
Regions:
Pacific Northwest, Washington State, Cascade Range
First Reported:
1980-05-18

Full Account

Batsquatch is a modern American cryptid associated with the volcanic and forested terrain around Mount St. Helens in Washington State. The name is a portmanteau of 'bat' and 'Sasquatch'. The creature is described as roughly nine feet tall with a humanoid, ape-like body, leathery bat-like wings, blue or purple fur, sharp teeth, yellow eyes, and a wolf- or dog-like muzzle. The most cited encounter is the April 1994 report by 18-year-old Brian Canfield, who said his pickup truck stalled on a remote road south of Mount Rainier and a winged figure landed in front of him before flying away; the Tacoma News Tribune ran the story and reporter C. R. Roberts is credited with coining the name 'Batsquatch'. Older popular accounts also tie the cryptid to the period following the 1980 eruption of Mount St. Helens, but contemporary 1980 reports are not well documented in mainstream sources.

Also Known As

Etymology

Origin:
Coined in print in 1994 by Tacoma News Tribune reporter C. R. Roberts following Brian Canfield's reported encounter, blending 'bat' with 'Sasquatch'.
Meaning:
Portmanteau describing a Sasquatch-like being with bat wings.
First Use:
1994, in C. R. Roberts's reporting in the Tacoma News Tribune (Washington)
