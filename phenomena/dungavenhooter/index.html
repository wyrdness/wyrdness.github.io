Dungavenhooter

cryptid Danger: unknown Evidence: unknown

Overview

A fearsome critter of North American lumberjack folklore: a mouthless, alligator-like swamp creature that pounds its victims to vapor with its tail and inhales them through its enormous nostrils.

Details

Origin:
Folkloric range across the swampy lumberwoods of the northeastern United States and the upper Great Lakes, broadly between Maine and Michigan as recorded in Tryon's 1939 catalogue.
Regions:
New England, Maine, Upper Great Lakes, Michigan, Wisconsin
First Reported:
1939

Full Account

The Dungavenhooter is a 'fearsome critter' from the lumber camps of the northeastern and Great Lakes United States. It is described as a large crocodylian beast with no mouth, instead possessing huge nostrils. From cover among brush, the Dungavenhooter ambushes lumberjacks — preferring those who have been drinking — and beats them into a vapour with its powerful tail before inhaling the resulting mist through its nose. The creature is recorded in Henry H. Tryon's 'Fearsome Critters' (1939), part of the small canon of published lumberwoods folklore that includes works by William T. Cox and Art Child.

Cultural Significance

Universally treated within the source tradition as a humorous tall tale; the 'belief' is performative, intended to entertain and to haze new arrivals to the camps.
