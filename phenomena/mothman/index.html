Mothman

cryptid Danger: unknown Evidence: unknown

Overview

A large winged humanoid creature with glowing red eyes reported in and around Point Pleasant, West Virginia from November 1966 through December 1967. Sightings clustered near the abandoned 'TNT area' of a former World War II munitions plant. The phenomenon culminated, in popular culture, in the December 15, 1967 collapse of the Silver Bridge.

Details

Origin:
Sightings centered on Point Pleasant, West Virginia and the immediate Ohio River Valley (Mason County, WV; Gallia County, OH; Meigs County, OH). A small number of contemporaneous reports came from elsewhere in the eastern United States but the canonical wave is local.
Regions:
West Virginia, Ohio, Ohio River Valley, Appalachia
First Reported:
1966-11-12

Full Account

Mothman is the name given to a winged humanoid figure reported by more than 100 witnesses across the Point Pleasant area of West Virginia during a 13-month period beginning November 15, 1966. The first widely-reported sighting was made by Roger and Linda Scarberry and Steve and Mary Mallette near the abandoned West Virginia Ordnance Works (locally called the 'TNT area'). Witnesses described a roughly 7-foot, grey, humanoid creature with a wingspan of 10 feet and large, intensely glowing red eyes. The name 'Mothman' was coined by an Ohio copy editor as a riff on the popular Batman television series. Reports continued through 1967 and ended after the catastrophic collapse of the Silver Bridge on December 15, 1967, which killed 46 people and bound the creature to a harbinger-of-disaster framing in subsequent folklore. Journalist John Keel's investigations, published as The Mothman Prophecies (1975), tied Mothman to a wider 'Fortean' wave of UFO sightings, Men in Black encounters, and the entity 'Indrid Cold'. Skeptical explanations include misidentification of large North American owls (especially the barred owl) and sandhill cranes, with the eye reflectivity attributed to the tapetum lucidum of nocturnal birds. The town has since embraced Mothman as a cultural icon, with an annual festival and a museum in downtown Point Pleasant.

Etymology

Origin:
The name 'Mothman' was coined by a copy editor at the Mason County Independent (or, in some accounts, an Athens, Ohio newspaper) drawing on the contemporary popularity of the Batman television series, which ran 1966–1968.
Meaning:
A portmanteau of 'moth' (for the wings) and 'man' (humanoid posture). Witnesses themselves had described 'a bird' or 'a man with wings'.
First Use:
1966-11 (Point Pleasant, West Virginia and surrounding press coverage)

Timeline

  • 1966-11-12 — Five gravediggers near Clendenin, WV report a brown human-shape flying low overhead (Earliest reported sighting of the wave)
  • 1966-11-15 — Scarberry/Mallette TNT-area encounter at ~midnight (Triggered the Point Pleasant Register article that started the press cycle)
  • 1966-11-16 — Point Pleasant Register publishes 'Couples See Man-Sized Bird... Creature... Something' (Anchored the initial reporting)
  • 1966-11-18 — Mason County Sheriff George Johnson holds press conference confirming reports (Lent local-authority credibility to the wave)
  • 1966-11 — Copy editor at Athens (OH) Messenger or similar coins 'Mothman' as a Batman riff (Names the phenomenon)
  • 1966-11-25 — Connie Carpenter encounter near Mason, WV (paralyzing red-eyed gaze) (Among the most-cited individual sightings)
  • 1967 — John Keel makes multiple investigative visits to Point Pleasant; Mary Hyre (local journalist) hosts him and tracks reports (Central documentary record of the wave)
  • 1967-12-15 — Silver Bridge (Point Pleasant ↔ Kanauga) collapses during evening rush, killing 46 (Mothman sightings end shortly after; the bridge collapse becomes the harbinger-narrative anchor)
  • 1971 — NTSB final report on Silver Bridge identifies an eyebar fatigue crack as the engineering cause (Provides the prosaic engineering explanation)
  • 1975 — Publication of John Keel's The Mothman Prophecies (Cements Mothman as a Fortean / harbinger figure in pop culture)
  • 2002 — Release of the film The Mothman Prophecies (Mark Pellington, dir.) (Reintroduces Mothman to mainstream audiences and drives Point Pleasant tourism)
  • 2003 — First annual Mothman Festival held in Point Pleasant (Locks in cultural-tourism status)
  • 2005 — Mothman Museum opens in downtown Point Pleasant; Bob Roach commissions the 12-foot Mothman statue (Robert Roach, sculptor) (Permanent civic monument)

Cultural Significance

Locally, Mothman has shifted from a frightening cryptid to a civic mascot, although the harbinger framing remains in folkloric retellings.
