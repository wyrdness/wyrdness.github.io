Phoenix Lights

ufo_uap Danger: unknown Evidence: unknown

Overview

A widely witnessed series of unidentified aerial lights observed across Arizona and parts of Nevada on the evening of 13 March 1997. Two distinct events occurred — a high-altitude V-formation moving south, and a later set of stationary lights over the Estrella Mountains south of Phoenix — both later attributed by U.S. Air Force investigators to A-10 Thunderbolt II aircraft and illumination flares from Operation Snowbird training exercises.

Full Account

The Phoenix Lights are a sequence of unidentified flying object reports made between roughly 7:30 p.m. and 10:30 p.m. Mountain Standard Time on 13 March 1997, observed across approximately 300 miles of southwestern airspace from southern Nevada through Phoenix to the Tucson area. Two separate phenomena were involved. The first, occurring shortly after 8 p.m., was a triangular or V-shaped formation of lights moving silently and slowly south over Arizona; tens of thousands of people are estimated to have seen it. Among the witnesses were the Ley family north of Phoenix, amateur astronomer Mitch Stanley in Scottsdale (who reported he saw individual aircraft through a telescope), and Arizona Governor Fife Symington III, who later said he saw an enormous craft. The second event, around 10 p.m., was a row of stationary lights over the Estrella Mountain range south-west of Phoenix. The U.S. Air Force later identified the first event as a formation of A-10 Thunderbolt II aircraft on a training flight returning to Davis-Monthan Air Force Base, and the second as illumination flares dropped by A-10s from the Maryland Air National Guard at the Barry Goldwater Range under Operation Snowbird; the flares hung beneath parachutes and appeared to fade out one by one as the Estrella ridge occluded them. Astronomer and retired USAF pilot James McGaha and others have published technical reconstructions consistent with this explanation. Robert Sheaffer has called the Phoenix Lights 'perhaps the most widely witnessed UFO event in history'. The case remains a touchstone of U.S. UFO discussion, particularly because of Symington's 2007 reversal in which he said he had personally witnessed an unexplained craft.
