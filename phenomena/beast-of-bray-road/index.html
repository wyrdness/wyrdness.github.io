Beast of Bray Road

cryptid Danger: unknown Evidence: unknown

Overview

A wolf-like, sometimes-bipedal cryptid reported near Elkhorn, Walworth County, Wisconsin, beginning in the late 1980s. Investigated and popularized by reporter Linda Godfrey, who later argued it is part of a wider 'manwolf' or 'dogman' phenomenon across the Midwest.

Details

Origin:
Reports center on rural Walworth County, Wisconsin (the Elkhorn / Lake Geneva area), with related 'manwolf' or 'dogman' reports across Wisconsin and the broader Upper Midwest.
Regions:
Upper Midwest, Wisconsin, Walworth County
First Reported:
1936-01-01

Full Account

The Beast of Bray Road is a cryptid reported in and around Elkhorn, Walworth County, Wisconsin, with most sightings clustering in the late 1980s and early 1990s. Witnesses describe a 6- to 7-foot-tall wolf-headed creature, covered in fur, with glowing red or yellow-orange eyes, sometimes seen standing or running on its hind legs and sometimes on all fours. Reports of contact with vehicles include claw marks on doors. Reporter Linda Godfrey, then writing for the Walworth County Week, was assigned to cover the story and produced a series of articles that became the 1992–1993 'werewolf of Elkhorn' coverage and her 2003 book The Beast of Bray Road. Godfrey, initially skeptical, became a long-time investigator of related 'manwolf' reports across Wisconsin, Michigan and the Midwest. Skeptics attribute the reports to misidentified gray wolves, large feral dogs, mange-affected bears, and a folklore feedback loop reinforced by a 2005 Asylum horror film and other media.

Also Known As

  • Wisconsin Werewolf (English, Wisconsin, USA)

Etymology

Origin:
Named for Bray Road, a rural road near Elkhorn in Walworth County, Wisconsin, where multiple sightings clustered in the late 1980s and early 1990s.
Meaning:
Literally, the beast of Bray Road; 'Bray' is the family name commemorated by the road, not a description of the creature.
First Use:
Around 1991–1992 in the Walworth County Week newspaper coverage by Linda Godfrey

Timeline

  • 1936-01-01 — Night watchman at St. Coletta School in Jefferson, Wisconsin reports a wolf-like creature digging on a mound — later associated with the cryptid (Earliest event commonly attached to the Beast of Bray Road tradition)
  • 1989-10-01 — Cluster of Bray Road sightings begins near Elkhorn, Wisconsin (late 1980s, exact dates uncertain) (Birth of the modern phenomenon)
  • 1991-12-29 — Linda Godfrey assigned by the Walworth County Week to investigate the local 'werewolf' reports; her articles are published over the following months (Brings the cryptid into print and into national cryptid media)
  • 2003-01-01 — Linda Godfrey publishes The Beast of Bray Road: Tailing Wisconsin's Werewolf (Foundational book on the phenomenon)
  • 2005-01-01 — The Asylum releases the direct-to-DVD horror film The Beast of Bray Road (Cements the cryptid in pop culture)
