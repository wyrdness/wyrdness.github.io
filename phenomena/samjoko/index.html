Samjok-o

mythological_creature Danger: unknown Evidence: unknown

Overview

A three-legged crow that lives in the sun, central to East Asian solar mythology. In Korea, the Samjok-o was a Goguryeo-era emblem of imperial authority and the sun, attested on 6th–7th-century tomb murals; cognate figures are the Chinese Sanzuwu / Jinwu and the Japanese Yatagarasu.

Full Account

The Samjok-o (삼족오, 三足烏) — literally 'three-legged crow' — is a solar bird shared across Chinese, Korean and Japanese mythology. The bird is said to live in the sun and to embody its power; in Chinese tradition, ten such crows once perched on the Fusang mulberry tree and took turns crossing the sky in Xihe's sun-chariot until the archer Hou Yi shot down all but one of them. In Korea, the Samjok-o is most strongly identified with the Goguryeo kingdom (37 BCE–668 CE), where it appears on multiple painted tomb murals of the 4th–7th centuries CE, often flanked by a dragon and a phoenix and explicitly representing the sun and the legitimacy of the Taewang ('Greatest of Kings'). Goguryeo and later Korean iconography treated the Samjok-o as superior in rank to both the dragon and the bonghwang (Korean phoenix). The Japanese Yatagarasu — described in the 8th-century Kojiki and Nihon Shoki as the divine crow that guided Emperor Jimmu from Kumano to Yamato — is a cognate figure, depicted with three legs in later art under Chinese influence and now used as the emblem of the Japan Football Association. The Samjok-o has been revived as a Korean cultural symbol since the 2000s, appearing in television dramas (notably 'Jumong' and 'The Legend'), corporate logos and national-identity discourse.

Etymology

Origin:
East Asian solar bird mythology shared between China, Korea, and Japan
Meaning:
삼 (sam, 'three') + 족 (jok, 'leg') + 오 (o, 'crow') = 'three-legged crow'
First Use:
Bird-Sun motifs documented archaeologically in China from the Neolithic period; the three-legged form is established by the Han dynasty and dominant in Goguryeo tomb art by the 5th–6th century CE

Timeline

  • 0168-01-01 — Han-dynasty Mawangdui silk painting depicting a sun with a crow inside it (datable to c. 168 BCE) (Among the earliest visual attestations of the sun-crow motif)
  • 0500-01-01 — Samjok-o appears on Goguryeo tomb murals (e.g. Ssang-yong-chong, Anak Tomb No. 3 cluster, Deokheung-ri, etc.) flanked by dragon and phoenix (Establishes the Samjok-o as a Goguryeo imperial symbol; date approximate (5th–7th c.))

Cultural Significance

The Samjok-o connotes solar power, divine authority, and dynastic legitimacy in Korean tradition.

Related Phenomena

  • Sanzuwu / Jinwu — Chinese three-legged sun crow; the direct cognate from which the Korean and Japanese forms derive
  • Yatagarasu — Japanese three-legged crow, divine guide of Emperor Jimmu
  • Bonghwang — Korean phoenix; frequently paired with the Samjok-o on Goguryeo tomb murals
  • Fenghuang — Chinese phoenix, structurally complementary to the Sanzuwu
  • Qilin / Kirin — Another auspicious East Asian mythical creature in the same iconographic family
