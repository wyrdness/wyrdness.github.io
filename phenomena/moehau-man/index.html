Moehau Man

cryptid Danger: unknown Evidence: unknown

Overview

A large, hairy bipedal cryptid said to inhabit the rugged Moehau Range and other forested ranges of New Zealand, often linked by Maori tradition to the maeroero, a wild bush-dwelling people.

Details

Origin:
Primarily the Moehau Range and broader Coromandel Peninsula of the North Island, New Zealand. In Maori tradition the maeroero are spoken of in remote bush regions across both the North and South Islands.
Regions:
Coromandel Peninsula, Moehau Range, North Island, Karangahake Gorge
First Reported:
Pre-contact

Full Account

The Moehau Man is a New Zealand cryptid associated with the Moehau Range at the northern tip of the Coromandel Peninsula on the North Island, and named after that range. Witnesses describe a tall, hairy, bipedal creature, and Maori tradition describes the maeroero (or maero) — a class of wild, hairy bush people of similar proportions and temperament. Some Maori commentators have suggested that modern Moehau reports describe a being descended from the maeroero. Mainstream New Zealand commentators have offered prosaic explanations: George Dean, then director of the Auckland Zoo, dismissed an 'escaped baboon' theory in 1969 on the grounds that such an animal could not survive in the New Zealand bush, while in 1970 Coromandel County Councillor Jim Reedy told researcher Robyn Gosset the 'Hairy Moehau' had grown out of an exaggerated joke and Bob Grey told her the term originally referred to a Yankee steam hauler used in logging. The phenomenon nonetheless persists in popular and Maori cultural memory.

Etymology

Origin:
Named after the Moehau Range on the Coromandel Peninsula. 'Maeroero' (also 'maero') is a Maori term for wild bush-dwelling beings.
Meaning:
Moehau is the place name; maeroero refers to bush-dwelling wild people in Maori tradition.
First Use:
The maeroero appear in long-standing Maori tradition; modern English-language 'Moehau Man' usage in 20th-century newspaper accounts.

Cultural Significance

In Maori tradition the maeroero are aggressive bush-dwelling beings to be avoided; the modern Moehau Man is sometimes interpreted as a continuation of this tradition.
