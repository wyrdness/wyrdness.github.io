Precognition

Overview

Precognition is the claimed ability to perceive or gain knowledge of future events without using known sensory channels or rational inference. Mainstream science classifies it as pseudoscience, citing the absence of replicable evidence and conflict with causality.

Details

Origin:
Precognition is reported across most human cultures and has been studied principally in laboratories in the United States and Western Europe.
Regions:
First Reported:
Antiquity

Full Account

Precognition (Latin prae-, 'before' + cognitio, 'acquiring knowledge') is the alleged paranormal ability to perceive future events directly. It is a subtype of extrasensory perception (ESP) studied historically within parapsychology, particularly through forced-choice card tests at J. B. Rhine's Duke University laboratory from the 1930s. Notable later work includes Helmut Schmidt's random-event-generator experiments in the 1960s and Daryl Bem's nine-experiment 'Feeling the Future' paper in the Journal of Personality and Social Psychology (2011, doi:10.1037/a0021524). Bem reported small effects consistent with precognition; pre-registered replications by Ritchie, Wiseman, and French (2012, PLOS ONE) and a large 2023 multi-lab pre-registered study failed to find an effect. Samuel Soal's 1940s ESP experiments, originally cited as supportive evidence, were shown by Betty Markwick (1978) to involve data manipulation. Mainstream scientific bodies and the broader psychological community treat precognition as pseudoscience: it conflicts with the causal direction of time in physics, has not produced replicable effects, and is plausibly explained by coincidence, confirmation bias, false memory, retrofitted prediction, and selection effects.

Knowledge before the event
First Use:
English usage attested from the 17th century; modern parapsychological sense from J. B. Rhine's writings in the 1930s

Timeline

  • 1882 — Founding of the Society for Psychical Research (SPR) in London (Beginning of systematic Western study of psychic phenomena including precognition)
  • 1934 — J. B. Rhine publishes Extra-Sensory Perception, presenting Zener-card experiments at Duke University (Foundational text of laboratory parapsychology and forced-choice ESP testing)
  • 1940 — Samuel Soal's ESP experiments begin in London (Initially cited as strong support for ESP/precognition; later shown by Markwick (1978) to involve data manipulation)
  • 1969 — Helmut Schmidt publishes precognition / PK experiments using electronic random number generators (Inaugurates an electronic-RNG era of psi testing)
  • 1978 — Betty Markwick demonstrates data tampering in Soal's experiments (Major historical correction to the parapsychological record)
  • 1995 — U.S. government Stargate Project (remote viewing) terminated; AIR review concludes operational use unjustified (Ends the most prominent state-funded psi-research program)
  • 2011-03 — Daryl Bem publishes 'Feeling the Future: Experimental Evidence for Anomalous Retroactive Influences on Cognition and Affect' in JPSP (Catalysed the modern psychology replication crisis and renewed methodological reform)
  • 2012-03 — Ritchie, Wiseman, and French publish failed pre-registered replications of Bem's experiments in PLOS ONE (Independent failure to replicate; widely cited in the replication-crisis literature)
  • 2012 — Galak et al. report seven non-replications of Bem's precognition effects in JPSP (Same-journal failed replications; reinforces null result)
  • 2023 — Large pre-registered multi-lab replication including ESP-proponent labs reports null results for precognition (Most rigorous independent test to date; null)

Cultural Significance

Precognition remains widely believed in popular culture, with surveys consistently finding that a substantial fraction of the public reports having had at least one apparent precognitive experience.
