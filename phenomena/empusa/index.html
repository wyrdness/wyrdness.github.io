Full Account

Empusa (Greek: Ἔμπουσα) is a shape-shifting female being in Greek mythology. Ancient sources, notably Aristophanes ('The Frogs', 'Ecclesiazusae') and Philostratus ('Life of Apollonius of Tyana', 1st century CE), describe her as one of the supernatural retinue of the goddess Hecate, sometimes identified with the goddess herself. She is famously depicted with a single leg of bronze (or brass) and a second leg in animal form — most often that of a donkey — giving rise to her epithets Onokole and Onoskelis ('donkey-footed'). Empusa is a shape-shifter, alternately appearing as a hideous monster meant to terrorize travellers and as a beautiful young woman who seduces young men in order to feed on their flesh and blood. By late antiquity 'empousai' came to designate a class of phantoms equated with lamiai and mormolykeia. The figure is often cited in modern scholarship as a precursor to European succubus and vampire traditions.

Etymology

Origin:
Ancient Greek; the standard folk etymology connects 'empousa' with 'em-' + 'pous' ('foot'), giving 'one-footed' or 'foot-on'.
Meaning:
Folk-etymologically read as 'one-footed', a reference to Empusa's distinctive single bronze leg.
First Use:
Attested in Athenian comedy of the 5th century BCE (Aristophanes, 'The Frogs', 405 BCE).

Cultural Significance

Empousai were considered nocturnal apparitions, particularly at crossroads sacred to Hecate, and were repelled by ridicule and sacred invocation.

Related Phenomena

  • Lamia — Greek female demon also associated with seducing and devouring young men, equated with empousai in late antiquity.
  • Mormolykeia (Mormo) — Greek female phantom used to frighten children, grouped with empousai in later sources.
  • Hecate — Goddess of magic and the night whose retinue includes Empusa.
  • Succubus — Medieval European female demon thought to descend from antique seducer-spirits including Empusa.
  • Vampire — Modern scholarship sees Empusa as a conceptual ancestor of European blood-drinking female revenants.
