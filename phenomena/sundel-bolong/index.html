Sundel Bolong

ghost_haunting Danger: unknown Evidence: unknown

Overview

The Sundel Bolong is a female ghost from Indonesian (primarily Javanese) folklore, defined by a large hole in her back; she is the spirit of a woman who died pregnant or in childbirth under shameful circumstances and takes revenge on men and abducts infants.

Details

Origin:
Indonesian folklore, centered in Java but present throughout the archipelago including Sumatra, Kalimantan, and other islands.
Regions:
Java, Sumatra, Kalimantan, Indonesian archipelago broadly
First Reported:
Pre-modern

Full Account

The Sundel Bolong is one of Indonesia's most distinctive female ghosts. Her name translates literally as 'prostitute/whore with a hole' in Javanese — 'sundel' (sundal) meaning prostitute or whore, and 'bolong' meaning hole — referring to the defining characteristic: a gaping cavity in her back, sometimes described as revealing internal organs or filled with maggots. She appears as a beautiful woman dressed in traditional white clothing (often a white kebaya) with long black hair flowing down her back, concealing the hole. She superficially resembles the pontianak or kuntilanak, fellow Indonesian female ghosts associated with pregnancy and death. According to the folklore, the Sundel Bolong is the spirit of a woman who died during pregnancy or childbirth under tragic or shameful circumstances — often as an unmarried woman, a victim of sexual violence, or a woman who died in disgrace. In one variant, the child was born posthumously, emerging through her back, creating the hole. Modern folklore scholars suggest the myth developed in Javanese culture partly to discourage prostitution during the Dutch East Indies colonial period. As a vengeful spirit, she targets men and children — reportedly able to castrate men who reject her and abduct newborns to replace her lost child.

Etymology

Origin:
Javanese Indonesian. The term developed in Java and spread throughout the Indonesian archipelago.
Meaning:
'Sundel' (from sundal) means prostitute or whore in Javanese/Indonesian; 'bolong' means hole or hollow. The name describes both her social status in death-narrative and her defining physical characteristic.
First Use:
Exact first written attestation unknown; tradition is pre-modern, existing in oral form before Dutch colonial documentation

Cultural Significance

The Sundel Bolong is understood in Indonesian folk belief as a genuine spiritual threat, particularly to men who travel alone at night and to unprotected newborns. She is simultaneously a figure of horror and of sympathy — a victim of social shame who became monstrous through the injustice she experienced.
