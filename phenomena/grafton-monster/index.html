Grafton Monster

cryptid Danger: unknown Evidence: unknown

Overview

A pale, headless humanoid creature reportedly seen by a local journalist near Grafton, West Virginia, on the night of 16 June 1964. The legend rests on this single newspaper-published account.

Full Account

The Grafton Monster is a West Virginian cryptid associated with a single, well-documented sighting in June 1964. On the night of 16 June 1964, journalist Robert Cockrell, a reporter for the Grafton Sentinel, was driving along U.S. Route 119 near the Tygart Valley River outside Grafton when he reported encountering a large, smooth, 'seal-like' figure roughly 7–9 feet (2.1–2.7 m) tall with no visible head. Cockrell published an account in the Grafton Sentinel; a 18 June 1964 follow-up reported teenagers searching the Riverside Drive area armed with flashlights, and a 19 June piece characterised the affair as 'spring fever'. No further widely accepted sightings have been reliably documented in the news record. Modern commentators frequently group the Grafton Monster with other West Virginia cryptids of the 1960s such as the Mothman of Point Pleasant (1966–1967) and the Flatwoods Monster (1952). The town of Grafton has since adopted the legend, hosting a Grafton Monster Festival from 2024 onward.
