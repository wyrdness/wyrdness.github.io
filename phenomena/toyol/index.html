Toyol

entity_spirit Danger: unknown Evidence: unknown

Overview

An undead infant familiar in Malay and Indonesian folklore, kept by a shaman or owner to steal money and small valuables for its master. Belief remains widespread across the Malay Archipelago.

Details

Origin:
Belief is found throughout the Malay Archipelago and the Malay-Chinese diaspora, including Malaysia, Indonesia, Singapore, Brunei, and southern Thailand.
Regions:
Peninsular Malaysia, Java, Sumatra, Borneo, Riau Archipelago
First Reported:
Pre-colonial

Full Account

The toyol (Malay) or tuyul (Indonesian) is a small, child-like spirit kept as a familiar by a bomoh, dukun, or pawang (shaman) for the benefit of a paying client. Traditionally described as resembling a near-naked toddler with greenish-grey or pale skin, sharp teeth, red eyes, and pointed ears, the toyol is set to tasks such as theft of small sums of money - tradition holds it 'steals half' rather than the whole, allowing the loss to go unnoticed. Other regional names include Javanese thuyul, Sundanese kecit, and the Hokkien Chinese-diaspora kwee kia. Belief is widespread across Malaysia, Indonesia, Singapore, and Brunei, persisting alongside official Islam (which deems such practices haram). Origins are sometimes attributed to deceased fetuses or stillborn infants, though this is folkloric rather than ethnographically uniform. The toyol is a recurring figure in Malay-language horror cinema (notably the long-running Toyol franchise) and in popular Indonesian and Malaysian television and literature.
