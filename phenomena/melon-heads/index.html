Melon Heads

urban_legend Danger: unknown Evidence: unknown

Overview

Regional American urban legends — concentrated in Michigan, Ohio and Connecticut — describing small humanoid beings with grossly enlarged heads who emerge at night from forested back roads, asylum sites, or rural mansions to attack trespassers.

Details

Origin:
Three principal regional clusters in the United States: Michigan (especially the Felt Mansion area in Saugatuck Township, Allegan County), Ohio (Kirtland in Lake County and Chardon Township in Geauga County), and Connecticut (southern Litchfield County, central/eastern Fairfield County, western New Haven County).
Regions:
Lower Michigan, Northeast Ohio, Western Connecticut
First Reported:
Late 20th century

Full Account

The Melon Heads (or Melonheads) are a cluster of regional American urban legends best documented in Michigan, Ohio and Connecticut. In each variant, witnesses claim to encounter small, pale, hairless humanoids with disproportionately large bulbous heads that lurk in wooded areas and along isolated rural roads. The Michigan version centres on the Felt Mansion area of Saugatuck Township, Allegan County, with origin stories invoking a fictional 'Junction Insane Asylum'; locally the beings are called 'wobbleheads.' The Ohio version is concentrated in Kirtland (Lake County) along Wisner Road and in Chardon Township (Geauga County), with a 'Dr. Crow' figure as villain. The Connecticut version is the broadest, spread across southern Litchfield County, central and eastern Fairfield County and western New Haven County, with two main origin stories: an asylum/prison-escape narrative and a Colonial-era witchcraft-banishment narrative. None of the alleged historical institutions corresponds to a real place: the 'Junction Insane Asylum' did not exist (the Felt Mansion area was at one point a prison), and 'Dr. Crow' / Crowe / Krohe is similarly unevidenced. The legends are commonly suggested to draw on misunderstanding of hydrocephalus and on real institutions such as Fairfield Hills State Mental Hospital and Garner Correctional Institution in Newtown, Connecticut.

Also Known As

  • Wobbleheads (English, Michigan, USA)
  • Melonheads (English)

Etymology

Origin:
Coined in mid- to late-20th-century American teen folklore, descriptive of the alleged enlarged 'melon-shaped' heads of the beings.
Meaning:
A pejorative American slang phrase comparing the head shape to a melon.
First Use:
Mid-to-late 20th century in regional teen oral tradition; entered local print sources by the late 1990s and early 2000s
