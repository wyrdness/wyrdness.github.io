Aka Manto

ghost_haunting Danger: unknown Evidence: unknown

Overview

Aka Manto ('Red Cloak') is a Japanese urban legend about a masked, red-cloaked spirit who haunts public and school toilets and asks the occupant to choose between red or blue toilet paper (or red or blue cloak). Either choice ends in death; only refusal lets the victim escape.

Details

Origin:
Originally a Japanese school-yard legend; widely retold across Japan and exported through pop culture to East Asia, North America, and beyond.
Regions:
Osaka, Tokyo, Kitakyushu, Korean Peninsula (under Japanese administration in 1940)
First Reported:
1930s

Full Account

Aka Manto (赤マント, 'Red Cloak'), also known as Red Cape, Red Vest, or in some regional variants Aoi Manto ('Blue Cloak'), is a long-standing Japanese urban legend told in schoolyards from at least the 1930s. According to the most common form of the tale, a masked male spirit wearing a flowing red cloak appears to people sitting on the toilet in a public or school bathroom — typically the last stall, late at night — and asks whether they want red paper or blue paper. Choosing red causes the victim to be slashed or flayed until their blood soaks through their clothes, producing the 'red cloak'; choosing blue causes them to be strangled or drained of blood until their skin turns blue. Naming any other colour brings a worse death (often being dragged into the floor or 'underworld'). Survival requires either ignoring the spirit or refusing both options. Variant forms swap the toilet paper for cloaks or capes, or substitute the Kainade legend in which a hairy yokai's coloured arm reaches up from inside the toilet. According to folklorist Matthew Meyer, accounts circulate from as early as the 1930s; one tradition places the legend's origin in an Osaka elementary school around 1935, with the legend reaching Tokyo within a year or two and spreading to Kitakyushu and Japanese-administered Korea by 1940. The historic word 'manto' originally referred to a sleeveless kimono-style jacket and only later came to mean a Western-style cloak, which has produced generational variation in retellings. Aka Manto sits within a network of Japanese school-toilet legends including Hanako-san, the Akaname, Teke Teke, and Kuchisake-onna.

Etymology

Origin:
Japanese; combination of 赤 (aka, 'red') and マント (manto, originally a sleeveless kimono jacket, later a Western-style cloak or cape).
Meaning:
'Red Cloak' or 'Red Cape'.
First Use:
School-yard rumour from at least the 1930s; the term 'manto' shifts in meaning across the 20th century, producing both 'red coat / vest' and 'red cloak / cape' descriptions of the entity.

Cultural Significance

Aka Manto is treated as a hostile spirit specifically tied to public and school toilets; standard 'protective' responses involve refusing both options or saying nothing.

Related Phenomena

  • Akaname — Yokai associated with dirty bathrooms
