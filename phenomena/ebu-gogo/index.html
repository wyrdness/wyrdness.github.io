Ebu Gogo

cryptid Danger: unknown Evidence: unknown

Overview

A small, hairy, humanoid creature in the oral traditions of the Nage people of central Flores, Indonesia, often discussed in connection with the discovery of Homo floresiensis at Liang Bua in 2003.

Details

Origin:
Folkloric range confined to central Flores Island, Indonesia, particularly the area around the Nage village of Boawae and the volcanic massif of Ebulobo.
Regions:
Flores Island, Central Flores, Ngada Regency, Nusa Tenggara Timur
First Reported:
20th century

Full Account

Ebu Gogo are diminutive, hair-covered humanoids in the oral traditions of the Nage people of central Flores Island, Indonesia. The name combines Nage 'ebu' (grandparent / elder) and 'gogo' (one who eats anything). They are described as roughly 1.5 m tall with broad, flat-nosed faces, long hairy bodies, and pendulous breasts in females; able to mimic human speech 'parrot-like'; and given to raiding gardens, kidnapping children, and consuming food crudely. Nage tradition holds that the Ebu Gogo were exterminated by the Nage people in the 18th century after the creatures stole and ate a child: the villagers gave the Ebu Gogo bundles of palm fibre as a 'gift', let them retreat into a cave, and then set the fibre alight. The 2003 discovery of Homo floresiensis at Liang Bua, also on Flores, prompted ethnologist Gregory Forth and linguist John McWhorter to suggest that Ebu Gogo traditions might preserve cultural memory of late-surviving relatives of the species. This hypothesis has little mainstream support, particularly after the species' extinction was redated to ~50,000 BP, but the case is widely cited as a folklore–palaeoanthropology bridge.

Etymology

Origin:
From the Nage language of central Flores: 'ebu' (grandparent, elder) + 'gogo' (one who eats anything / glutton).
Meaning:
'Grandparent who eats anything' or 'glutton elder'.
First Use:
Recorded in 20th-century ethnographic work on the Nage; given international prominence by Gregory Forth's writings from the 1990s onward.

Timeline

  • 1998-01-01 — Gregory Forth publishes 'Beneath the Volcano: Religion, Cosmology and Spirit Classification among the Nage' (Foundational ethnography of Nage cosmology including Ebu Gogo material)
  • 2003-09-01 — Mike Morwood and team discover Homo floresiensis remains at Liang Bua, Flores (Triggers international interest in Ebu Gogo as possible cultural memory)
  • 2004-10-28 — Brown et al. publish 'A new small-bodied hominin from the Late Pleistocene of Flores, Indonesia' in Nature (Formal scientific description of Homo floresiensis)
  • 2008-01-01 — Gregory Forth publishes 'Images of the Wildman in Southeast Asia: An Anthropological Perspective' (Sets the Ebu Gogo within a comparative Southeast Asian wildman framework)
  • 2016-03-30 — Sutikna et al. publish revised Liang Bua stratigraphy redating Homo floresiensis extinction to ~50,000 BP rather than ~12,000 BP (Substantially reduces plausibility of an 18th-century Ebu Gogo / H. floresiensis link)
  • 2022-01-01 — Gregory Forth publishes 'Between Ape and Human: An Anthropologist on the Trail of a Hidden Hominoid' (Forth's most direct statement of the hypothesis that Ebu Gogo may correspond to an extant or recently extinct unknown hominoid)

Cultural Significance

Nage tradition treats the Ebu Gogo as having been historically real ancestors-of-place; modern Nage informants generally regard them as extinct since the 18th century.
