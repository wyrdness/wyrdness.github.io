Orang Bati

cryptid Danger: unknown Evidence: unknown

Overview

A reported winged humanoid creature from Seram Island in the Maluku (Molucca) Islands of eastern Indonesia, described as approximately five feet tall with bat-like black wings and red skin, said to emerge from caves on Mount Kairatu to abduct children from remote villages at night.

Details

Origin:
Reported exclusively from Seram Island (Ceram), Maluku (Molucca) Islands, eastern Indonesia. The creature is specifically associated with the interior mountains, particularly Mount Kairatu (Gunung Binaiya).
Regions:
Seram Island, Maluku Province, Indonesia, Mount Kairatu (Gunung Binaiya) area
First Reported:
1500-01-01

Full Account

The Orang Bati ('men with wings' in Malay/Indonesian) are reported from the mountainous interior of Seram Island, the second-largest island in the Maluku archipelago of eastern Indonesia. According to indigenous Alfur community traditions, the creatures inhabit a network of caves on the slopes of Mount Kairatu (also called Gunung Binaiya, 3,027 m), emerging nocturnally to raid isolated mountain villages and abduct children. Physical descriptions are consistent: approximately five feet tall, with black leathery bat-like wings, red or reddish skin, and a long thin tail. Witnesses report hearing a long, mournful wail before attacks. The tradition appears to predate both Islamic and Christian missionary contact, as Christian missionaries from the 15th and 16th centuries recorded hearing accounts of a winged monster terrorizing the village of Uraur. In 1987, British missionary Tyson Hughes arrived on Seram for an eighteen-month contract and encountered these beliefs directly; he documented local accounts and claims to have personally witnessed a creature. Scientific investigation by television host Richard Terry concluded the creature was most likely the large flying fox (Pteropus vampyrus), which can reach wingspans of up to 1.5 meters and emits piercing screams. No physical specimen, photograph, or verifiable physical evidence has been documented. The Orang Bati remains unverified.

Also Known As

  • Orang-bati (Malay/Indonesian, Seram Island, Maluku, Indonesia)

Etymology

Origin:
From Malay/Indonesian orang (person, people) and bati (winged; from a root meaning to fly or have wings). The name is used by indigenous communities of Seram Island and was adopted in broader Indonesian usage.
Meaning:
Men with wings; winged people
First Use:
Pre-contact Alfur oral tradition of Seram Island; documented in writing by Western sources in the 15th–16th century missionary accounts

Cultural Significance

Indigenous Alfur communities of Seram Island maintain genuine fear of the Orang Bati, with specific protocols for child safety during nighttime and village warning systems. The tradition persists alongside both Islamic and Christian religious frameworks adopted across Seram.
