Overview

Garuda is a divine eagle-like bird in Hindu and Buddhist mythology, the mount (vahana) of Vishnu, king of birds, and eternal enemy of the nagas (serpents); it serves as the national symbol of Indonesia and Thailand.

Full Account

Garuda is a divine bird creature central to Hindu and Buddhist mythology. As the mount (vahana) of the god Vishnu, Garuda represents courage, strength, and vigilance. He is depicted in two primary forms: a giant eagle-like bird (zoomorphic) or an eagle-headed human with wings and talons (anthropomorphic). When depicted with human characteristics, Garuda may have an eagle-like beak or nose, with an emerald-colored body and golden-yellow wings. Traditional iconography describes four color bands: golden yellow from feet to knees, white to the navel, scarlet to the neck, and black above. In Hindu tradition, Garuda is the son of the sage Kashyapa and Vinata; his mother was enslaved after losing a bet, and Garuda waged war against the gods to obtain the nectar of immortality (amrita) to free her. This act established his role as protector and his eternal rivalry with the nagas, who guard amrita. In Buddhism (as Garula), Garudas are golden-winged birds among the eight classes of inhuman beings, natural enemies of nagas. In Jainism, Garuda serves as a yaksha or guardian for the tirthankara Shantinatha. Garuda is the national emblem of Indonesia (Garuda Pancasila) and Thailand (Khrut), appears on their currencies and official seals, and gives its name to Indonesia's national airline, Garuda Indonesia. The Garuda Purana, one of the eighteen Mahapuranas of Hinduism, bears his name. He is mentioned in the Rigveda under the name Garutman.

Etymology

Origin:
Sanskrit; the name Garuḍa (गरुड) is ancient, appearing in the Rigveda
Meaning:
Possibly from the Sanskrit verb gri (to speak); scholar George Williams proposes this derivation. Also connected to the Greater adjutant stork (Leptoptilos dubius) in some traditions.
First Use:
Rigveda (composed c. 1500–1200 BCE); Garutman appears as a celestial deva in the oldest stratum

Cultural Significance

In Vaishnava Hinduism, Garuda is the vahana (vehicle) of Vishnu and is venerated as a deity in his own right. Temples to Garuda exist across South and Southeast Asia. In devotional practice, invoking Garuda provides protection against snake bites and poison.
